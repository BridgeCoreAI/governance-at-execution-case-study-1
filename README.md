## Overview

Governance at Execution™ Case Study 01 examines whether a specific consequential AI execution remains admissible under the material conditions applicable when execution is attempted.

The case study distinguishes general authorization from execution-specific admissibility and examines what happens when conditions change between an earlier approval, evaluation, or execution and a later consequential action.

The central governance question is:

> Determine whether a specific consequential AI execution remains admissible under the material conditions applicable when execution is attempted.

---

## Why This Matters

An AI system may be approved, a user may be authorized, and a prior execution may have been permitted.

Those facts alone do not establish that a later consequential execution remains admissible.

Governance at Execution™ shifts the governance question from:

> Was this system approved?

to:

> May this specific execution proceed now?

## MVAC v1.0

Case Study 01 uses MVAC v1.0 as the execution-context model.

MVAC contains six components:

### Principal
Who or what is attempting the consequential execution?

### Action
What consequential action is being attempted?

### Object
What data, resource, content, or asset is being acted upon?

### Target
Where or toward what is the action directed?

### Current State
What material conditions apply when execution is attempted?

### Authorization Context
What authority permits or constrains the principal and action under the applicable conditions?

Prior execution history is not a seventh MVAC component. Its material consequences are represented through the applicable existing components.

---

## Key Findings

Case Study 01 established the following conceptual findings:

1. Prior authorization does not automatically establish current execution admissibility.

2. Admissibility is execution-specific.

3. Material uncertainty should not silently become permission.

4. Execution-time governance depends on sufficient and relevant evidence.

5. The governance decision must remain bound to the execution that actually occurs.
